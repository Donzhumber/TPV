3. ABSTRACT (88 words)
This paper develops a dynamic indirect mechanism to identify rational types in unknown environments characterized by observational pooling. The central theoretical result, the Rational Type Identification Theorem, proves that the principal's Bayesian beliefs converge almost surely to the agent's true latent type along prolonged interactions without direct revelation reports. This asymptotic identification is operationalized by the Mano de Dios-Guadalupe process, an endogenous maximum-entropy noise kernel that ensures full support across actions within a competing-risks architecture. A complementary Implementability Theorem guarantees execution in Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium. Microeconometric calibration and deep-learning simulations of Colombian kidnapping validate rapid type separation and policy feasibility.

Dear Professor Forest,

It is a great privilege to submit the manuscript entitled "Identifying Rational Types in Unknown Environments under an Indirect Dynamic Mechanism" for consideration for publication in Terrorism and Political Violence.

Given the distinguished international reputation and interdisciplinary leadership of Terrorism and Political Violence in publishing rigorous research on the strategic, behavioral, and organizational dynamics of political violence, insurgency, and terrorism, we believe the journal provides the ideal venue for this study.

In conflict and counter-terrorism settings—specifically involving armed insurgent organizations such as the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC), the National Liberation Army (ELN), and United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC) paramilitaries—strategic interactions are characterized by pervasive information asymmetry, clandestine operations, and observational pooling. Standard direct revelation mechanisms break down because violent non-state actors possess powerful incentives to conceal or misrepresent their latent operational types, capabilities, and objectives.

This paper develops a novel dynamic mechanism design framework tailored to the strategic and behavioral dynamics of terrorist and insurgent organizations under observational pooling. Grounded in microdata from Colombia's Centro Nacional de Memoria Historica (CNMH) spanning over four decades of conflict, the manuscript makes four central theoretical, computational, and policy contributions directly aligned with the scope of Terrorism and Political Violence:

1. Dynamic Indirect Mechanism for Insurgency Screening: Rather than relying on abstract direct communication schemes that are unworkable in violent conflict environments, we formalize an indirect dynamic screening mechanism. The state (principal) deploys observable counter-terrorism and counter-kidnapping instruments, while insurgent groups (agents) best-respond dynamically based on private financial pressure, ideological commitment, and logistical vulnerability.

2. Stochastic Implementation via the Mano de Dios–Guadalupe (MDG) Process: To capture tactical frictions, intelligence failures, and operational hazards in high-stakes conflict environments, the MDG process endogenously generates execution noise under maximum-entropy constraints. This ensures full support across action paths, sustaining well-defined off-path Bayesian updating and guaranteeing that the interaction is implementable in Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium (PBE).

3. The Rational Type Identification Theorem and Insurgent Typology: The core theoretical contribution establishes that under recurrently separated observable signals (captivity duration and competing terminal hazards: military rescue, ransom payment, escape, execution), trajectory measures across distinct insurgent types become mutually singular. Consequently, the counter-terrorism planner's Bayesian posterior beliefs converge almost surely to the true latent type of the insurgent cell (separating opportunistic criminal factions from disciplined ideological guerrillas).

4. Counter-Terrorism Policy Design (alpha vs. gamma): Using microeconometric competing-risks duration models and deep-learning neural network value approximators, the paper evaluates the structural trade-offs between financial interdiction (alpha, freezing illicit financial flows and ransom assets) and operational pressure (gamma, military and police tactical interventions). We establish the policy frontier governing captive survival, extraction efficiency, and conflict duration, showing how aggressive military intervention can perversely accelerate hostage executions unless properly calibrated with intelligence-driven financial pressure.
